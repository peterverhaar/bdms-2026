							38 Torrington Square, 
							London W.C. 1, 
							April 1, 1928.

Dear Mr. Woolf, 
		You may think it rather extraordinary
that I should ask you for any help, but things have 
come out very unhappily for me. 

		I think that the thing that really
weighed most with me in January and caused all my vacil-
lation and consequent wrong decision was the fact that
I had previously had some dealing with another firm. 
I might want to accept it, and that I ought not to 
accept your post with that possibility in my mind. I
can't think now why I ever thought I might prefer it
and I have regretted a hundred times both my indecision
and my over-scrupulousness. 

		Anyhow, after keeping me in suspense for
weeks (it is months since the first conversation), they
have finally decided to promote someone inside the firm
instead (just what I might have expected) and the whole
thing falls to the ground, a great disappointment. 

		This means that I have to begin again to 
canvass all the firms in London, which is very disheart-
ening. It is so difficult to know the right moment. 

		What I thought I would ask you is this. 
As you are inside the publishing world, you might pos-
sibly hear in advance that some firm was contemplating
changes or expansion. An application under those cir-
cumstances would stand a much better change of receiving
attention. If you ever did hear of such a thing and
would let me know, I should be immensly grateful, and
would not use your name at all, if you did not wish it. 
I hope you will not think that I am taking too great a
liberty in asking you. 

			Yours sincerely
			C. K. Cumming
