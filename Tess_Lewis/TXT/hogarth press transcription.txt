W.L.I
Dec 7th 1933
Dear Mr. Woolf, 
I regret extremely that the Hogarth Press has had to write to me so often about the account. I was sent to this country to study law but with my own mind set on [illegible] [illegible] and politics worked for the money to publish the pamphlet and got it in as much as you had accepted the manuscript [check for coherence?]. But I received no encouragement to be allowed to live in London and try to write. In April I received one or two offers of articles about the W. L. [illegible] team; and realizing that perhaps here was a chance to avoid the law, which I desire intensely, I took the risk and came into London on my own with the [L. 16#] I know I should have paid, but it was my only way out. I have [prowresses?] of looking next summer from the Manchester [illegible]. But the winter has been rather a blank. I am sure, however, that I shall raise the money before the end of the month; but it is comparatively speaking a large sum and it will take me a little time. That is why I [wanted?] to see you a few days ago. I would not want you to think that, the book being published, I neglected the matter. 
Yours sincerely, 
C.L.R. James.
