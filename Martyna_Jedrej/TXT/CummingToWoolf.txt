----- Letter from C. K. Cumming to Leonard Woolf -----

[1]

38 Torrington Square,
London, W.C. 1,
January 24, 1928.

Leonard Woolf, Esq.,
The Hogarth Press,
52 Tavistock Square, W.C. 1.

[stamp, unintelligible]

Dear Mr. Woolf,

Thank you very much for your letter of the 20th. January, offering me the post of Manager. I am sorry not to have replied before, but I have been giving the question very careful consideration.

The idea has appealed to me strongly and I felt last week that the fascination and interest of the work would outweigh other considerations. I like many-sided work, have a passion for detail, and do not object to a considerable amount of drudgery. But, as I thought about the matter, I became doubtful, for reasons that I would like to explain, if I may.

I should feel that I ought to do a considerable amount of supplementary work, if I could get it. Unfortunately, the people I wished to consult, seem to have all removed themselves from London for the moment, and will not be back until next month. That makes an immediate decision more difficult. But I am rather afraid that with the hours what they are, it would not be possible to accomplish the requisite amount in any event. If the post were a part-time one - even two-thirds time, 10 to 4 - it would be a different matter.

Knowing myself, I am certain I should never settle down permanently on a salary of [unclear: £4] a week for full time, unless I saw great possibilities in the post itself, which is just what it is impossible to say without experiment. If I undertook the work on the proposed terms, the arrangement would, in my mind, be frankly experimental. There is this further point. I have a number of friends in book publishing circles and I have thrown out various lines, from which it is possible, though not probable, that some offer might result. If, during the first few months, a better post should unexpectedly be offered me, I should, in all likelihood, want to accept, for I could not afford to do otherwise. I should wish to have the option of leaving at one month's notice (which is, in my view, the usual and most satisfactory period), if I so desired.

What I have said is, I know, tantamount to declining the post, for you naturally would wish to make arrangements practically certain to be permanent. I feel sure that you will appreciate

[2]

my point of view and the fact that I have tried to be perfectly candid. I felt that to accept the post with any idea in my mind that, for the reasons indicated above, I might not wish to remain permanently, would not be fair to you. I am very sorry indeed for any delay or waste of time that I may have caused you and I want to thank you for so kindly offering me the work, which would, in itself, be highly congenial in every way.

Yours sincerely

[handwritten signature, partly unclear: C. K. Cumming]
