29 December 1947Gentlemen,You have been so kind as to give my collaborator, Professor Reginald E. Watters of the University of British Columbia, permission to reprint in an anthology of essays intended for college English classes Virginia Woolf’s “How Should One Read a Book?” Mr. Watters has also obtained permission from Harcourt, Brace & Company, American publishers of The Second Common Reader, in which an essay with this title appears. As you no doubt know, however, this essay had an earlier form, which appeared in the Yale Review in 1926 and was reprinted in The Yale Review Anthology in 1942. For our purposes this earlier form is more desirable than the later form, which, as you remember, came at the end of The Second Common Reader and assumed acquaintance with what had been said in the preceding essays of that book. The 1926 form of Mrs. Woolf’s essay has the greater independence and is therefore more teachable in such an anthology as ours.May we have your permission therefor to reprint the essay as it stood in the Yale Review rather than in The Second Common Reader? I am venturing to address you instead of Mr. Watters as I am nearer both to you and to our publishers, who are Scribner’s. In order that I may have prompt reply I enclose 18 cents in international reply coupons, and I beg that you will use them to reply by air mail. If you grant this permission, we will of course make acknowledgement as you direct, either with or without mention of The Second Common Reader. The payment we asked was four guineas, and Harcourt, Brace asked $40. We wish to obtain distribution rights for both the United States and Canada.Yours sincerely,Benfield PresseyProfessor of English
