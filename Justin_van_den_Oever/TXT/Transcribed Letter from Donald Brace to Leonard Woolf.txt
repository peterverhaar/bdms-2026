								August 10, 1928.

Mr. Leonard Woolf,

52 Tavistock Square,
London, W.C.L. Eng.

Dear Mr. Woolf:

	I have your letter of July 28th referring to the 
fourth clause in the agreement with Mr. Gaige. Your 
understanding of Mr. Gaige’s intention is substantially 
correct. The entire edition will be printed with Mr. Gaige’s 
imprint, and of this edition Dulau will take 200 copies for 
sale in England. The fourth clause is intended to express 
two things: first, Mr. Gaige’s obligation to get this 
edition out so as not to interfere with or delay the 
publication of the book by you and ourselves; second, the 
requirement that I have insisted on, that his edition should 
do nothing to cloud the copyright. Technically, the limited 
edition must be published or sold simultaneously in the two 
countries; prior publication in America alone would probably 
invalidate the British and European copyright, and it is 
Mr. Gaige publishes in America, the copyright will be 
effective in both countries. I think this is important for 
all of us. The situation is, therefore, that there will be 
no publication in England in the sense of having a publisher 
and a separate imprint and all that, but that sales will take 
place as a protection to you.

	To this limited extent there is a question about 
the date of publication in England, and it seems to me an 
important one. I hope this explains the matter.

							Yours sincerely,

									Donald C. Brace.

DCB.
