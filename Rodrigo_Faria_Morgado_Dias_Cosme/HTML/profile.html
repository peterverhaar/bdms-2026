About me

Hello! My name is Rodrigo, I'm 23, and I'm from Portugal.

In my free time, I like to read (I know, right? so original), play video games, binge a good TV show, and watch football (even though I can barely kick one). And, most importantly, I have an undying love for Ben & Jerrys :)

I used to hate reading when I was younger, and this continued well into my teenage years. It wasn't until a couple of years ago when my sister lent me Agatha Christie's And Then There Were None that I realised I could easily sprint through the pages; it was just a matter of finding the right book and the right genre. Since then, I've been reading mostly murder mysteries (e.g., Richard Osman, Nita Prose, Benjamin Stevenson, etc.), and lately I've explored dystopians (love these!), thrillers, and a bit of sci-fi.

 

As I said, I was born in Portugal. However, at the age of 5, my parents, my sister, and I moved to Copenhagen, Denmark ('cause the weather is soooo much better up there...). I grew up there and quickly picked up on the language.

At 14, we moved back down south - this time to Galicia in northwestern Spain, which is where I completed my high school studies in a British school, where I became fluent in Spanish (and obviously improved my English). That's where I live now (except for the Netherlands, of course) with a cat and a dog.

BA Digital Society

I completed my previous education in sociology at Maastricht University in a programme called Digital Society. It's an unusual name, and all the students there have always struggled to explain it properly. It's an interdisciplinary programme focusing on the fields of politics, ethics, philosophy, technology, governance, big data, etc. - a little bit of everything. In short, it focuses on how the digitalisation of societal processes and constructs affects society as a whole (doesn't sound abstract at all, right?). It was a lot of fun and extremely interesting.

I finished my bachelor thesis in June. I studied the effects of location-tracking apps (Life360, Find My, etc.) on children's privacy and independence from their parents. Before my thesis, I was also a Communications Intern at a technology lab in the university, where my role was web design, content creation, and social media management. It's called The Plant, and I learned a lot and was surrounded by the best team of people imaginable.

Maastricht

My time in Maastricht started a year before I joined my bachelor programme. After high school, I initially enrolled in a different programme, BSc Data Science and Artificial Intelligence. However, as any data science student will tell you, it wasn't exactly enjoyable. I decided to switch gears (towards the humanities and sociology) but maintain my focus on technology, which is where my BA fit right in.

Life in Maastricht was incredible! Most people there are students, so you really feel the vibe of university life. It's a small town, so you easily learn to find your way around surprisingly fast (I never once got lost walking home drunk from the other side of the city).

Recommended books

Fahrenheit 451

Ray Bradbury (1953)

I wasn't a big fan of the ending, but the book overall is a real eye-opener for fans of dystopian books. The story takes place in a reality where books have been banned, and it follows the life of a 'fireman' tasked with burning books reported to the authorities.

Dungeon Crawler Carl

Matt Dinniman (2020)

This one is incredibly well-written and definitely one of my favourite books. I know the cover looks goofy (and I definitely tend to judge books by their covers myself), but I gave it a try, and it was absolutely worth it.

Current read

Project hail Mary

Andy Weir (2021)

I finally caved under peer pressure and started reading this, but it's taking me unusually long. The plot is really interesting, but the writing is too technical. Still recommendable, though.
